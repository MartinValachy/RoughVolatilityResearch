# S01 - targeted evidence card

This is a targeted methodological audit, not a claim that every source section has been fully checked.

## Citation

Gatheral, Jaisson & Rosenbaum (2018), Volatility is rough, Quantitative Finance 18(6), 933-949; DOI 10.1080/14697688.2017.1393551.

## Version and status

Downloaded arXiv 1410.3394, October 2014 author version (41 PDF pages). Journal identity verified on publisher page; do not transfer figure numbers between versions.

## Inspected source locations

PDF pages 7-8: observation definitions; pp 19-21: finite-lag comparison and simulation; Section 5: forecasting not fully re-audited.

## Model and assumptions

Log volatility follows a fractional OU/RFSV model with H<1/2 and slow mean reversion; small-time scaling is interpreted through moments of log-volatility increments.

## Observation and error layer

DAX/Bund use a renormalized 10-11 am London uncertainty-zones estimate; S&P/NASDAQ use whole-day Oxford-Man realized variance. These are proxies for spot quantities, not directly observed spot volatility.

## Estimator and target

Log moment versus log lag regressions at multiple powers q, then scaling exponent versus q; forecasting framework uses fractional Gaussian history.

## Data and horizon

DAX/Bund 1248 days, 13 May 2010-1 Aug 2014; Oxford-Man index sample 3540 days, 3 Jan 2000-31 Mar 2014 (this author version, pp 7-8).

## Source-grounded finding

The paper finds rough scaling and motivates RFSV. Its own simulation explicitly shows integration-window regularization: reported H about 0.16 for the one-hour estimator and about 0.18 for an eight-hour estimator when input H=0.14 (p 21).

## Limitations and unresolved audit

These empirical scaling fits do not identify a latent mechanism independently of proxy quality. This local corpus is an early version; journal revisions and exact forecast equations need separate reconciliation.

## Our replication status

No GJR figure claimed reproduced. Our Level B toy is separate, lacks uncertainty zones and uses Brownian OU truth.

## Relevance and defensible extension

Treat low H as established descriptive evidence. Separate observation operator, latent regularity and forecast target; test finite-grid alternatives without claiming their idea is new.
