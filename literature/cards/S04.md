# S04 - targeted evidence card

This is a targeted methodological audit, not a claim that every source section has been fully checked.

## Citation

Bolko, Christensen, Pakkanen & Veliyev (2023), A GMM approach to estimate the roughness of stochastic volatility, Journal of Econometrics 235(2), 745-778; DOI 10.1016/j.jeconom.2022.06.009.

## Version and status

Downloaded arXiv 2010.04610 author version dated 7 September 2022, 54 PDF pages. Publisher identity/DOI verified; publisher OA version is separate from our hashed author PDF.

## Inspected source locations

PDF pages 12-15: exact-no-leverage RV error, Table 1, corrected moments(37)-(42) and identification condition; p 29 Table 3.

## Model and assumptions

Lognormal fractional stochastic volatility and moment conditions for integrated variance; H can range on both sides of 1/2.

## Observation and error layer

Noisy IV proxy with zero-mean serially uncorrelated measurement error; exact RV error moments under independent return and volatility drivers, plus CLT approximations. Their c(theta) corrects the zero-lag second moment.

## Estimator and target

GMM compares sample moments with G(theta)+(0, c(theta), 0, ...). Measurement-error variation is modeled as a function of structural parameters instead of a free nuisance offset (pp 13-14).

## Data and horizon

Oxford-Man leading equity indices and high-frequency simulation; exact date/intraday-count information is in Table 3, varying by index.

## Source-grounded finding

Noise correction is central, and the journal abstract still reports estimatedH around 0.05. This is counterevidence to the claim that correcting sampling error necessarily produces BrownianH.

## Limitations and unresolved audit

Assumption 4 imposes identification; a full-rank local Jacobian is not global identification in a nonlinear model (p 15). Exact finite-n RV derivation excludes drift/leverage; bipower CLT under roughness is explicitly qualified (p 13).

## Our replication status

Moment formulas inspected, no GMM fit or table replication claimed. Online supplement acquisition remains unresolved.

## Relevance and defensible extension

A credible primary estimator is more demanding than subtracting an arbitrary constant. Preserve finite-n observation construction and identification assumptions.
