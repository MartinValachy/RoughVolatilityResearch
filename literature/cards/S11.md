# S11 - targeted evidence card

This is a targeted methodological audit, not a claim that every source section has been fully checked.

## Citation

Bollerslev, Patton & Quaedvlieg (2016), Exploiting the errors: A simple approach for improved volatility forecasting, Journal of Econometrics 192(1), 1-18; DOI 10.1016/j.jeconom.2015.10.007.

## Version and status

49 PDF-page 2015 CREATES working paper rp 15_14; publisher identity/DOI verified, not the journal layout.

## Inspected source locations

PDF pages 6-7 eqs 1-5: IV/RV/IQ/RQ; p 11 eq 13 basic HARQ; pp 11-12 illustrated noisy-day response. p 7 visually inspected.

## Model and assumptions

HAR with a daily autoregressive coefficient depending on square-root quarticity; fullHARQ lets additional lag coefficients vary.

## Observation and error layer

Finite-sampling RV=IV+eta, variance approximately 2 IQ/M, with RQ=(M/3)sum r^4. No price-noise-robust variance formula is obtained by merely relabelingRQ.

## Estimator and target

Basic level HARQ: RV_t=beta 0+(beta 1+beta 1Q*sqrt(RQ_{t-1}))*RV_{t-1}+beta 2*weeklyRV+beta 3*monthlyRV+u_t (working-paper eq 13).

## Data and horizon

S&P500 and DJIA constituents plus simulations. Exact primary empirical replication not attempted.

## Source-grounded finding

Measurement precision changes the predictive weight placed on recentRV; basic HARQ is itself a noise-aware forecasting comparator.

## Limitations and unresolved audit

Log-HARQ must follow an explicitly audited specification; replacing labels and retaining the same coefficient formula is not an automatic source-faithful implementation. RQ scale and actual sampling count matter.

## Our replication status

Quarticity identities verified numerically. HARQ fitting intentionally held until pilotRQ units/session M are resolved.

## Relevance and defensible extension

Use this established benchmark before attributing rough-model gains to a latent mechanism.
