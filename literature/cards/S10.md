# S10 - targeted evidence card

This is a targeted methodological audit, not a claim that every source section has been fully checked.

## Citation

Corsi (2009), A Simple Approximate Long-Memory Model of Realized Volatility, Journal of Financial Econometrics 7(2), 174-196; DOI 10.1093/jjfinec/nbp001.

## Version and status

23 PDF-page journal copy from WU faculty teaching materials; original author/publisher links also registered. Copyright retained; local reading only.

## Inspected source locations

PDF page 4 /journal 177 definitions 2-4; p 6 /journal 179 transformation caveat; Section 2.3 HAR construction.

## Model and assumptions

Parsimonious heterogeneous autoregression using daily/weekly/monthly averages; its motivation is a cascade of components at different trading horizons.

## Observation and error layer

Original exposition uses square-root realized variance, annualized; explicitly permits analogous variance or log variants (p 6). Average volatility is not the square root of average variance.

## Estimator and target

Linear HAR regression. Our direct log average-future-variance baseline is a declared variant and uses horizon-specific training-only smearing.

## Data and horizon

Source empirical details not fully audited for replication; no original data claim.

## Source-grounded finding

Provides the benchmark family and emphasizes that approximate persistence need not imply literal long memory.

## Limitations and unresolved audit

Transformation, annualization, aggregation and direct-horizon choices must be explicit. The name log-HAR alone does not fix all conventions.

## Our replication status

Baseline implemented and run in a separate OU daily toy; no claim to reproduce original coefficients.

## Relevance and defensible extension

A transparent benchmark with correctly matured labels and positive level forecasts.
