# S06 - targeted evidence card

This is a targeted methodological audit, not a claim that every source section has been fully checked.

## Citation

Bennedsen, Christensen & Christensen, To be or not to be: Roughness or long memory in volatility?, arXiv 2403.12653, revised author text January 2026.

## Version and status

60 PDF pages. Cover indicates revisions following referee input, but exact journal bibliographic mapping remains unresolved; do not call this downloaded version a published 2026 article.

## Inspected source locations

PDF pages 1-5: MCLE scope and limitations; pp 6-7: composite likelihood; pp 36-37: discussion; p 45: moment benchmarks.

## Model and assumptions

Stationary Gaussian fOU and generalized Cauchy dependence, with distinct short-/long-run behavior.

## Observation and error layer

Intraday log spot-RV proxies remain noisy; the paper explicitly notes potential estimator distortion and studies log trading volume as related evidence (p 5, p 37).

## Estimator and target

Composite likelihood based on low-dimensional marginal/conditional Gaussian densities; asymptotic rate and limit shape depend on memory and whether the mean is known (p 3).

## Data and horizon

Main empirical application is cryptocurrency high-frequency data, excluded from our own empirical scope; methods may inform the equity design.

## Source-grounded finding

The authors find decoupled short-/long-run correlation structure useful, while explicitly recognizing RV proxy limitations.

## Limitations and unresolved audit

Their fixed-spacing long-span framework does not validate daily OLS slope standard errors. Memory can change inference; no generic block bootstrap guarantee follows.

## Our replication status

Full text acquired; targeted methods audit only; no MCLE code or empirical result reproduced.

## Relevance and defensible extension

Require separate persistence controls and assess dependence-specific inference instead of importing an off-the-shelfH interval.
