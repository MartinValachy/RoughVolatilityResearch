\begin{abstract}
This paper studies how historical trade-source information propagates into realized-volatility diagnostics and constrained portfolio inputs. A controlled comparison covers \Fact{sessions} historical sessions for SPY, with a fixed follow-up extending four ETFs to the same calendar: \BF{unionPairs} distinct source-paired fund-days in total. For SPY, the same strict literal trade-condition filter produces materially different one-minute realized variance on \Fact{rvN60} of \Fact{den60} non-discovery dates. Finite-band slope differences do not identify latent market roughness. A fixed EWMA rule produces a persistent one-minute risk-input discrepancy, while the five-minute exposure cap suppresses \Fact{attenuation300}\% of the uncapped mean gap. Broader asset coverage qualifies that reassuring five-minute result: QQQ has \BF{QQQ300large} of \BF{QQQ300origins} dependent origins with a capped gap of at least five percentage points, against \Fact{large300} for SPY under the same original policy. Conversely, EFA's one-minute mean gap falls from \BF{EFA60uncapped}pp before the cap to \BF{EFA60capped}pp after it. Missing TLT grids withhold an incomplete-history comparison. The practical contribution is a reproducible audit separating source information, measurements, state estimates and constraints, with independent numerical checks and explicit historical/exploratory limits. It is not a new estimator, untouched-regime validation or a trading-performance claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Realized variance is often treated as an input to subsequent statistical and financial analysis. Before it becomes such an input, however, it is constructed from a sequence of choices: the source representation, the retained trades, the trading session, the treatment of simultaneous observations and the sampling grid. Applying the same code to two sources does not necessarily preserve the same information. A further complication arises when the resulting estimates enter a constrained decision rule. Different estimates can produce nearly identical portfolio weights if both weights reach the same cap.

This paper asks a deliberately narrow question: \emph{how do differences in source time and condition information change measured volatility, a finite-band roughness diagnostic and a fixed risk input, when the rest of the construction is held constant?} The question is investigated using two historical WRDS TAQ representations and controlled combinations of their fields. A small known-truth simulation provides context for interpreting the roughness diagnostic; it is not presented as a new discovery.

Rough-volatility research motivates the distinction between a latent process and its observed proxy. Gatheral, Jaisson and Rosenbaum~\cite{gjr} provided influential evidence and modeling arguments for rough volatility. Cont and Das~\cite{contdas} demonstrate how realized-volatility roughness can arise under nonrough latent dynamics. Fukasawa, Takabatake and Westphal~\cite{ftw} show that observation error can create misleading scaling while developing a different, observation-aware inference procedure whose empirical conclusions still support roughness. These results do not license a conclusion that every low empirical slope is an artifact. They motivate checking what the statistic measures.

Source sensitivity is also established prior art. Holden and Jacobsen~\cite{holden} document substantial liquidity-measure differences between Monthly and Daily TAQ. VOLARE~\cite{volare} discusses source-format, timestamp and odd-lot differences in realized-measure construction. The present paper therefore does not claim to discover that timestamps, filtering or measurement error matter. Its narrower contribution is to document a controlled source-information mechanism for realized variance and quarticity, then trace that mechanism through a simple state estimate and an exposure constraint.

For SPY, the initial mechanism finding is that the frozen strict filter selects different information across the source formats. At one minute, changing flags while retaining precise time preserves much of the observed discrepancy, whereas using modern flags with legacy time produces much smaller incidence. Restoring modern category-B information largely removes the one-minute downstream weight gap under this particular pipeline. This is a conditional mechanism diagnostic, not a recommendation to remove every B-coded trade.

The practical finding is equally important: small constrained weight gaps need not imply similar risk estimates. The original five-minute mean weight gap is only \Fact{gap300} percentage points (pp), but becomes \Fact{uncapped300}pp before the cap. A systematic post-result sensitivity reports every fixed policy setting and separates absolute decision gaps from scale-free state comparisons. This makes the study a useful undergraduate case study in reproducible empirical finance, while keeping the economic claims modest.

The fixed four-fund follow-up makes this lesson more specific. Five-minute sampling does not eliminate the proposed-weight discrepancy for QQQ under the same original policy, while EFA exhibits substantial cap masking even at one minute. This heterogeneity is reported with every specified fund and grid, including missing comparisons, rather than used to select a favorable asset. The extension was specified after the SPY result but before the additional acquisition.

\section{Data and controlled design}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Sample, provenance and discovery}
The original A10 sample contains every fixed XNYS session from 2 January 2013 through 31 July 2014 for SPY: \Fact{sessions} sessions. The fixed January 2014 panel additionally contains QQQ, IWM, EFA and TLT. Together, that original source-paired export has \Fact{symbolDays} symbol-days and \Fact{records} matched ordered input records before cleaning. These are correspondences of observed rows, not a verified count of unique exchange executions; repeated identical records can be indistinguishable.

A13 subsequently extends all four ETFs over the same \BF{sessions}-session calendar, adding \BF{newPairs} paired fund-days and reusing the \BF{reusePairs} previously seen January pairs. The union with SPY contains \BF{unionPairs} distinct fund-days, without counting reused observations twice. Its separate primary incidence calendar excludes the previously seen January month as well as the fixed discovery dates; it is not identical to the original SPY incidence denominator. Section~\ref{sec:breadth} and Appendix~\ref{app:breadth} define and report this follow-up.

The mounted historical sources are a legacy seconds representation and a millisecond representation. Their common available overlap determines this case-study window. The source extraction is performed on WRDS; raw ticks are not downloaded. Source subsets are checked for matching ordered price, size and venue observations before comparison, with no invented joins used to force alignment. Local exports contain daily measures, diagnostics and provenance. Matching observed records conditions the comparison on a shared observable input sequence; it does not prove that either source is a complete or error-free record of economic trades.

The mechanism was first investigated on two dates, 2 January 2014 and 3 July 2014. The expanded crossed-source design was fixed before its full run, after that discovery and earlier training exploration. Primary incidence excludes these two explicit discovery dates, leaving \Fact{den60} SPY pairs at one minute. This exclusion is not advertised as an untouched chronological holdout. Full-window slope and EWMA-path calculations retain both dates. The later policy-grid exercise is explicitly post-result sensitivity. Validation observations from 2017--2019 and the final period from 2020 onward remain closed.

\subsection{Five source constructions}
The reference construction, denoted $M$, uses modern time and modern flags. Four alternatives are considered: legacy time and legacy flags ($L$); modern time and legacy flags; legacy time and modern flags; and legacy time/flags with modern category-B information restored ($B$). All retain the same remaining measurement pipeline. The restoration changes which information is available to the frozen literal filter; it is not adopted as a production cleaning rule.

The NYSE specification distinguishes condition meanings by tape: category B is average-price for CTA issues but bunched for UTP issues~\cite{nyse}. A single economic interpretation should therefore not be applied to all ETFs. Moreover, a source specification describes intended fields, not a proof of how a particular mounted derivative was generated. Direct floor-time and correction comparisons were additionally made on the two discovery dates; that specific stronger check is not silently generalized to every expanded-sample day.

\subsection{Analytical questions and study logic}
The study follows a chain of three questions. Does source representation change a measurement when the recorded price/size/venue sequence and remaining code are held fixed? Does that change alter a descriptive scaling statistic or a causal risk state? Does apparent agreement between capped inputs persist when the cap is removed? The controlled constructions address the first question; the fixed-band statistic and fixed EWMA address the second; the complete A11 matrix addresses the third. The design avoids choosing a forecasting model or policy because it happens to favor one source.

The prior expectation is a mechanism, not a desired sign: selecting different observations or changing their ordering within a grid can alter squared and fourth-power increments. Neither source must be universally larger, and smaller constrained gaps need not imply more accurate risk. Materiality thresholds define a transparent diagnostic of disagreement rather than a test of economic optimality. A10's engineering relevance criterion was fixed before its expanded analysis; the later A11 exercise explains a qualification discovered after the original results. These roles are kept distinct throughout.

\section{Measurement and evaluation methods}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Trading-session construction and daily moments}
The fixed pipeline retains in-session positive finite price and size observations, applies its uncorrected-trade rule, and keeps only whitespace-normalized blank or @ condition strings. It selects the dominant retained venue by trade count, breaking ties by venue identifier, then takes the median price at simultaneous timestamps. Previous-tick prices are sampled on six nested grids: 15, 30, 60, 300, 900 and 1,800 seconds. The exchange calendar supplies the session length, including early closes.

There is one explicit opening convention: if no retained observation occurs exactly at the open, the opening anchor uses the first eligible trade and the approximation is flagged. Subsequent unpopulated grid points are not filled backwards from that observation. An incomplete grid retains its expected/observed counts and missing moments rather than being treated as a shorter complete session. Thus a complete recorded grid does not imply a perfectly observed economic opening price. This convention is a limitation shared across the controlled constructions.

The strict blank-or-@ rule is an intentional literal-filter diagnostic. It is narrower than the inspected \texttt{highfrequency} cleaner's condition defaults and differs in venue-selection order~\cite{highfrequency}. It is not a full replication of a general BNHLS trade-cleaning pipeline, and the results should not be interpreted as an out-of-box package comparison.

For a complete grid with $M_t$ within-session log returns $r_{t,i}$, define
\begin{equation}
 \RV_t=\sum_{i=1}^{M_t}r_{t,i}^2,\qquad
 \RQ_t=\frac{M_t}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{M_t}r_{t,i}^4.\label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{app:moments} derives both normalizations from Gaussian increment moments and explains the role of actual session counts. Appendix~\ref{app:noise} distinguishes price noise from daily log-proxy noise. These models give interpretive benchmarks, not assumed descriptions of the source discrepancy.

Returns are in decimal units and the moments are unannualized. The session excludes a separately constructed overnight return. A common change to percent returns multiplies RV by $100^2$ and RQ by $100^4$; guessed rescaling cannot resolve incompatible definitions. Numerical tolerances for independent implementation checks are separate from the materiality thresholds below.

\subsection{Source discrepancy and finite-band roughness}
For positive valid source pairs and a moment $Q\in\{\RV,\RQ\}$, the discrepancy is $d_{t,Q}=\log(Q_t^{\mathrm{candidate}}/Q_t^M)$. RV is material when $|d_{t,\RV}|>\log(1.1)$; RQ is material when $|d_{t,\RQ}|>\log 2$. These are engineering thresholds, not significance tests or equivalence margins. Missing/invalid pairs do not count as agreement, and valid denominators are measure- and grid-specific.

For $x_t=\log\RV_t$, define the lag-$k$ empirical increment moment and finite-band statistic
\begin{equation}
 \widehat V(k)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal I_k|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_k}(x_t-x_{t-k})^2,
 \qquad \widetilde H_K=\tfrac12\operatorname{slope}_{k=1,\ldots,K}
       \{\log k,\log\widehat V(k)\}.\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal I_k$ retains valid endpoints on the original calendar. Missing dates never compress the lag. The primary band is $K=10$, with $K=5,22$ as fixed sensitivities; paired slope differences use common endpoint support. Equation~\eqref{eq:H} is descriptive. It is not Cont and Das's normalized variation estimator or Fukasawa et al.'s quasi-likelihood estimator, and no theorem for either is transferred to it.

\subsection{A fixed next-session risk input}
To study propagation without selecting a forecasting model, use the initialized EWMA state and constrained weight
\begin{equation}
 s_0=\RV_0,\qquad s_t=\lambda s_{t-1}+(1-\lambda)\RV_t,
 \qquad u_t=\frac{v_*}{\sqrt{252s_t}},\qquad w_{t+1|t}=\min(c,u_t).\label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
End-of-session $t$ data can supply an input for $t+1$; future returns are not used. The original settings are $\lambda=0.94$, $v_*=0.10$ and $c=1$. Excluding the first 126 observations leaves \Fact{origins} evaluated origins. State paths require complete positive history; a gap is not imputed or removed to make the recursion run. The annualization is a scenario convention for this session-based input, not a claim to model full overnight-inclusive portfolio risk.

Appendices~\ref{app:state}--\ref{app:weights} derive the initialized convolution, persistence of a source discrepancy, inverse-square-root sensitivity, cap threshold and scale-free source ratio. These derivations explain why one episode can affect many successive origins and why a common cap can conceal different states.

The analysis compares weight gaps, durations and input turnover. It does not evaluate returns, transaction costs, realized risk, forecast superiority or profit. A predefined practical warning criterion is met if at least 2\% of valid non-discovery days are material at a grid, or at least ten post-warmup origins have an absolute weight gap of at least 5pp. The criterion classifies engineering relevance and is not a novelty test.

\subsection{Uncertainty and post-result sensitivity}
The incidence intervals use paired-date stationary resampling~\cite{politis} with 3,999 draws. Mean block length 22 is primary; 11 and 44 are fixed sensitivities. The same resampled dates carry the numerator and valid-denominator indicators, with discovery dates ineligible for primary incidence. ETFs are not pooled as independent observations. The displayed percentile intervals describe resampling uncertainty under the specified dependence assumptions. They do not supply a uniform coverage guarantee under source-regime change, which is a material limitation of this historical window.

After seeing the original results, the A11 exercise fixes all 27 combinations of $\lambda\in\{.90,.94,.97\}$, $v_*\in\{.05,.10,.15\}$ and $c\in\{.5,1,2\}$. All settings and constructions are reported; no favorable policy is selected. Before capping,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{u_t^{L}}{u_t^{M}}=\sqrt{\frac{s_t^{M}}{s_t^{L}}},\qquad
 |\min(c,a)-\min(c,b)|\leq |a-b|.\label{eq:cap}
\end{equation}
The uncapped ratio removes the target and common annualization convention. The second relation shows mechanically why a common cap can suppress disagreement. This sensitivity is useful but is not a new confirmatory sample or independent replication.

\section{Known-truth context: a measurement diagnostic}\label{sec:simulation}
The supporting simulation separates model regularity from the observed statistic. A stationary Gaussian log-variance process $X$ is transformed to positive variance as $v_t=10^{-4}\exp(X_t-0.25/2)$. The OU case has covariance $0.25\exp(-\kappa|h|)$, with $\kappa=\log(2)/22$. Two generalized Cauchy covariance settings, following the covariance family discussed by Bennedsen, Lunde and Pakkanen~\cite{blp}, have local $H=.1,.3$ and the same 22-day half-correlation scale. They are stationary Gaussian Cauchy-covariance processes, not asserted to be fractional OU processes.

For each model, 128 independent paths have 504 sessions and 780 fine steps per session. Left-point variance values generate conditionally Gaussian log-price increments $\sqrt{v_j/780}\,Z_j$. Independent log-price noise of standard deviation 0, 1 or 3 basis points is added at fine-grid price endpoints; nested coarse returns sum the resulting increments. Daily grid-integrated variance is the mean of the fine variance values. Sampling intervals are 1, 5, 15 and 30 minutes, with the same fixed lag bands used for interpretation. The simulator is deliberately controlled and not calibrated to a full market microstructure process.

In the no-added-noise OU setting, local $H=.5$, while mean finite-band slopes from daily spot samples, grid-integrated variance and five-minute RV are respectively \Fact{ouSpot}, \Fact{ouIV} and \Fact{ouRV}. The Monte Carlo SE of the last statistic is \Fact{ouSE}. Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic} shows the observation-stage and grid dependence. This is an implementation-backed explanatory example, consistent with established concerns about proxy roughness~\cite{contdas,ftw}; it does not establish the latent process behind SPY or refute rough-volatility models.

\section{Why exposure agreement can be misleading}\label{sec:cap}
The original five-minute constrained mean gap is \Fact{gap300}pp. Before capping, it is \Fact{uncapped300}pp at the same target. Both sources are capped on \Fact{bothCap300}\% of evaluated origins, and the cap reduces the mean absolute discrepancy by \Fact{attenuation300}\%. Even at one minute the cap attenuates the mean gap by \Fact{attenuation60}\%. Equation~\eqref{eq:cap} makes this result mechanically understandable: both inputs can be mapped to the same constrained weight despite different states.

This qualification does not erase the observed grid difference. Across the three fixed decays, one-minute uncapped weight ratios exceed a 1.1 factor on \Fact{relativemin60}--\Fact{relativemax60} of \Fact{origins} origins. Five-minute counts are zero. The transformed p95 comparisons in Figure~\ref{fig:cap} use uncapped ratios, independent of the target, and remain smaller at five minutes. These are relative comparisons of two estimated states, not measurements of true risk or future discrepancy probabilities.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:limits}
The results distinguish three questions that can otherwise be conflated. First, do sources preserve the same information under a reused literal filter? Second, do the resulting measurement/state estimates agree? Third, do constrained decisions agree? Matching the last object cannot certify the first two. The combination of crossed source constructions and an explicit exposure-cap diagnostic is a practical audit workflow for empirical finance.

The study meets its predefined engineering relevance criterion, but that is a modest conclusion. It does not establish academic priority, a new estimator or a production-optimal cleaning rule. Existing work already establishes measurement-induced apparent roughness and historical TAQ compatibility problems. The value here is the dataset-specific controlled evidence, transparent source pipeline and independently checked propagation into a decision input.

Several limitations constrain generalization. The sample is historical and determined by the mounted common source overlap. Source regimes, timestamp precision, condition coverage and market structure may change. The expanded ETF panel shares the same historical source regime and cannot resolve these concerns. Stationary date-block intervals should therefore be read cautiously rather than as unconditional guarantees. The source reference is not verified integrated variance, and observed-row correspondence is weaker than unique execution identity.

The strict filter and opening convention are also consequential. The filter excludes conditions retained by broader cleaning defaults, while the dominant-venue and simultaneous-price choices interact with the source representation. The B-restoration exercise identifies a narrow mechanism only within this design. No silent deletion, threshold adjustment or guessed quarticity rescaling is used to create agreement. A future cleaning-rule comparison would be a separately specified study, not an interpretation retrofitted to this one.

Finally, the risk comparison is intentionally not an economic backtest. It uses fixed states and scenario weights, no fitted model-selection contest, and no returns, costs, cash, overnight or execution accounting. The smaller five-minute SPY discrepancies do not generalize uniformly across the extended funds and do not establish the best sampling interval. The original registered 1,250-day forecast comparison remains deferred for source/history feasibility reasons and is not presented as completed by this case study. An option implied-volatility surface is not required to answer the paper's realized-measurement question.

\section{Verification, reproducibility and disclosure}\label{sec:reproduction}
Verification is layered rather than asserted universally. The source export checks file identity, fixed sample coverage and per-construction measurement-check records. The moment pipeline compares SQL aggregation with an independent NumPy construction, including expected/observed grid counts. An independent calendar check can reject a shared early-close error that source agreement alone would miss. Matching ordered records is checked separately from numerical RV/RQ agreement.

At the result level, independent standard-library arithmetic recomputes \Fact{checksA10} A10 scalars. The A11 check uses original paired inputs, direct EWMA convolution and explicit shock propagation rather than the analysis recurrence and closed-form influence masses. It checks \Fact{checksA11} scalars and \Fact{overlaps} overlapping original-A10 values, and rejects a planted altered policy output. Synthetic tests cover selected covariance, scaling, missingness, initialization, causality and boundary cases. Appendix~\ref{app:audit} maps the checks to components and their limits. These checks support the recorded calculations; they do not establish economic truth, undocumented vendor semantics or the absence of every bug.

The supporting derivations have separate checks using exact Gaussian polynomial quadrature, Gamma-function moments, numerical differentiation of log variograms, initialized convolution and cap boundary cases. These verify selected identities under their stated assumptions; they do not verify that the assumptions hold for the empirical data.

The paper's numerical macros and tables are generated from recorded outputs and have a separate machine-readable claim map. A paper-specific independent checker verifies their source selectors, transformations and printed values. The source code, input/output hashes and run IDs support a concrete restart path. A separate synthetic-only demo/candidate runs without WRDS access or vendor records. Restricted daily data and this private empirical working history are not automatically public-release material.

\textbf{Data availability.} The empirical inputs are licensed historical WRDS source data. Raw ticks and vendor observations are not redistributed. The accompanying repository supplies processing source, synthetic examples and the manuscript. Reproducing the empirical findings requires separately licensed inputs, which the repository does not distribute. The repository omits private research history and source observations. Closed validation/final observations have not been used.

\section{Conclusion}
A source-compatible pipeline must preserve information, not merely reuse the same literal filter. In this fixed historical comparison, time/condition representations materially change realized measures and descriptive roughness statistics, and those differences propagate into a persistent one-minute risk-input episode. A common exposure cap can then hide disagreement between the underlying state estimates. The uncapped SPY check qualifies its smaller five-minute effects; the fixed ETF follow-up further shows that this sampling-frequency reassurance is asset-dependent. QQQ retains sizable five-minute proposed-weight gaps, while EFA illustrates strong one-minute cap masking and incomplete TLT grids remain withheld.

For applied work, the defensible lesson is to audit source fields, session counts, measurement statistics, uncapped states and constrained inputs as separate objects. The evidence supports that practical workflow. It does not select a universally best estimator, identify latent market roughness or demonstrate a profitable strategy.
